# Cross-subject quality comparison

## Evidence boundary

The local KooBits research report captured on 20 October 2026 directly maps the Science topic `Living & Non Living Things: Real Life` to the lesson `How do we know that robots are non-living things?` and the video `Science KooClass Living&Non Living Real Life KCV01 0010`. That is a same-topic comparison.

For English, the report exposes the public `KooBits Spelling Bits` product demo, but explicitly labels it `Not a lesson`; private English curriculum videos were not exhaustively represented. The English proof therefore compares the evidenced spelling interaction grammar, not unseen proprietary lesson content.

## Grounded quality assessment

All three proofs now improve on passive explanation in the same measurable ways: a learner predicts before feedback, the narrated referent is the active visual target, answers remain hidden during response windows, and the lesson ends with a new transfer item rather than repeating the worked example. Every delivered video is deterministic 1920×1080 H.264 at 30 fps with local narration and timed captions.

The strongest current advantage is instructional control, not cinematic spectacle. The proofs can precisely synchronize highlighting, manipulation, pauses, and feedback to narration. The Science proof also makes the claim depend on a visible evidence matrix; the English proof makes the actual letter transformation manipulable rather than decorative.

These renders are not yet evidence that the pipeline is categorically “better than KooBits.” The report does not provide a complete English lesson comparison, and no child study or blinded educator scoring has been run. Visually, the current style is clean and legible but still closer to premium motion graphics than an immersive animated world.

## Next quality gate

Run a blinded rubric against matched KooBits videos with at least three educators and a small learner sample. Score concept correctness, narration/action synchronization, attention guidance, answer leakage, transfer success, visual engagement, and recall. The next production iteration should then add richer character performance, camera staging, environmental continuity, and sound design only where those changes improve the measured learning outcome.
