# The Place Value Machine — Storylines

## Recommendation: The Brass Workshop

Choose this direction because the machine makes place value physical: each
digit visibly travels one column to the left, so the cinematic action is also
the mathematical explanation.

### 1. The Brass Workshop — object is the explanation

**Device and form:** A child operates a room-sized place-value machine in one
continuous workshop journey. Match cuts follow two glowing number tokens as
they travel through brass-and-glass columns marked ones, tens, and hundreds.

Mira welcomes Ari into a warm inventor's workshop and offers the number 23 as
two physical digit tokens. Ari pulls a multiplication lever, the machine moves
both digits one place left, and a quiet zero lamp illuminates the empty ones
place; after Ari mistakes this for “just adding a zero,” Mira runs 47 through
the same mechanism and lets Ari explain the real rule.

**First two seconds:** A copper lever marked ×10 snaps downward in Ari's hand;
behind it, the glowing digit 3 launches left along a glass rail.

**Beats:** lever and moving digit → reveal workshop and Mira → load 23 → every
digit shifts left → 230 lands → “just add zero?” misconception → load 47 and
pause → 470 lands → Ari states the rule → machine settles.

**Look:** 3D-inspired painted animation, warm walnut and aged brass, teal glass
shadows, amber number light, 35 mm framing, tactile controls and grounded
character contact.

**Sound:** restrained orchestral marimba and pizzicato pulse, mechanical clicks,
glass chimes for digit movement, a low brass settle for correct placement, and
clear close-miked dialogue.

## Alternative: The Night Train

### 2. Platform Ten — journey through a world

**Device and form:** Place-value columns are railway platforms. A tiny train
carries the digits, and every cut follows the train through the same lateral
screen direction.

Mira and Ari stand at Platform Ones with passengers 2 and 3. The ×10 train
moves every passenger exactly one station left: 3 reaches Tens and 2 reaches
Hundreds, leaving Ones empty; the return journey with 4 and 7 becomes Ari's
practice problem.

**First two seconds:** A teal locomotive bursts through steam, its destination
board reading ×10 while the camera tracks alongside the glowing digit 3.

**Beats:** train arrival → board 23 → passengers board → one-stop move → station
board becomes 230 → Ari's “zero carriage” mistake → 47 boards → viewer pause →
470 arrival → station lights spell the rule.

**Look:** moonlit storybook station, deep indigo sky, polished enamel signs,
warm carriage windows, rain-softened reflections, lateral camera travel.

**Sound:** rail rhythm as tempo, ticket-punch clicks, steam, bell motifs and a
gentle night-travel score.

## Bold alternative: The Shadow Theatre

### 3. Giants on the Wall — scale becomes value

**Device and form:** A hand-built shadow theatre continuously transforms one
frame. Moving each digit toward the lamp makes its projected value ten times
larger.

Ari arranges cut-paper digits 2 and 3 in front of a lantern. Mira rotates a
three-position stage marked ones, tens and hundreds; the digits shift one
position toward the light and their projected forms grow, while a circular
empty-place marker appears where Ones used to be. Ari tests 47 and discovers
that the rule is movement by place, not decoration with a zero.

**First two seconds:** A tiny paper digit 3 crosses a beam and its shadow grows
tenfold across a linen screen.

**Beats:** growing shadow → reveal tabletop theatre → arrange 23 → rotate stage
→ projection resolves to 230 → tear-away false zero gag → arrange 47 → pause →
projection resolves to 470 → two hands close the theatre.

**Look:** handcrafted paper, linen, wood and visible fingerprints; amber lantern
light against cobalt shadows; macro lens, shallow depth, practical transitions.

**Sound:** paper movement, wooden detents, breathy flute, hand percussion and a
soft room tone, with dialogue staged like an intimate bedtime story.

## Locked choice

The proof film uses **The Brass Workshop**. It offers the clearest physical
cause-and-effect, the strongest recurring world, and the most direct bridge
between cinematic character action and exact deterministic overlays.

## Production assumptions

- Runtime: 60 seconds.
- Format: 1920×1080, 30 fps, 16:9.
- Audience: children around age 9–11.
- Characters: Mira, a calm inventive teacher; Ari, a curious learner.
- Dialogue and captions are required.
- All numbers, operators, column labels, and worked arithmetic are deterministic
  graphics. Generative imagery must contain no instructional text.
- The film is approved only after mathematical, visual, audio, and educator QA.
